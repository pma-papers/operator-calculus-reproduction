# Canonical-operator assembly: exact law-level numerical checks

This design is fixed before calibration and independent validation outcomes are generated. It is motivated by the manuscript revision, not externally preregistered. It tests the quantities in the operator calculus directly, rather than comparing named optimization packages. Earlier common-panel and restart data remain preserved; this protocol does not retune or replace them.

Coverage amendment before full calibration or validation: the initial broad-Gaussian design was preserved in checkpoint `379885e`. The coverage is now expanded to include a second, clustered population geometry for every instance and assembly. This change is motivated analytically by the different effect of midpoint recombination on a broad cloud and on two separated clusters, not by calibration or validation outcomes; neither full phase has been run. Both families are retained throughout, and their calibration envelopes are pooled rather than separately selected.

## Operators and complete matrix

Use all ten existing problem/dimension instances (seven objective families), with exactly the same objective formulas and rotations. Every objective has known reference minimum zero. Each input law has 32 uniformly weighted points. Use both of the following population geometries for every instance: a broad cloud of independent N(0.6 times the all-ones vector, 1.2 squared times identity) points, and independent points from the mixture 0.5 N(+ones,0.1 squared times identity) + 0.5 N(-ones,0.1 squared times identity). Each mixture component is drawn independently per point; its sign is shared across that point's coordinates. The same two geometries are used without objective-specific modifications, including rotations of the objective.

Mutation is the law of x + tau b(x) + sigma sqrt(tau) Z, with standard Gaussian Z. Let g_FD be the coordinate central-difference gradient of radius 0.001. Set b(x) = -chi_R(x) g_FD(x)/sqrt(1+||g_FD(x)||^2), where R = 10 sqrt(d). The smooth radial cutoff is one on the radius-R ball and zero outside the radius-2R ball. In its transition, for u=||x||/R-1 in (0,1), it is h(1-u)/(h(u)+h(1-u)), where h(v)=exp(-1/v) for v>0 and zero otherwise. This is part of the declared operator, not a trajectory-dependent repair. Its participation is recorded.

Selection exactly reweights the finite law by exp(-tau omega Phi(x)), normalized to mass one, where Phi(x)=f(x)/(1+f(x)). Recombination replaces a fraction gamma tau of the law by the midpoint of two independent parents drawn from its input law. Thus the parent law has the required compatible marginals.

Retain all eight assemblies omega,gamma in {0,1} and sigma in {0,0.2}. Mutation's drift is present in every assembly; toggling sigma distinguishes its exploratory diffusion contribution. No configuration is selected or removed using its observed performance. Test tau=0.1/2^l for l=0,...,7. Compare M composed with R composed with S (selection first) and M composed with S composed with R (recombination first). Mutation is last in both, allowing an exact Gaussian objective expectation. This is a test of first-order order-independence, not a claim that finite steps commute.

The bounded selection pressure, smooth compactly supported drift, constant Gaussian diffusion, and midpoint mixing meet the canonical coefficient requirements. Gaussian noise and affine recombination have fourth moments. All test objectives and their first three derivatives have fourth-order polynomial growth or better, so q=4 is the relevant moment order for their objective-gap test. Compatibility does not imply negative objective drift; all signed component actions are retained.

## Exact law diagnostics and cost accounting

Selection and recombination produce support contained in the original 32 points plus their 32 squared pairwise midpoints. Compute the objective expectation after mutation analytically: polynomial second/fourth Gaussian moments for quartic, wells and Rosenbrock terms; damped trigonometric moments for the other objectives. Orthogonal rotations preserve the isotropic Gaussian covariance. Verify these formulas against independent Gaussian quadrature, and verify analytic gradients and Laplacians with finite differences.

For each input law, save its mean objective gap V, the separate drift, diffusion, selection and recombination actions, their sum g, and the exact output mean gap V_out for every step and order. The selection contribution is -omega Cov(Phi,f); it is not an uncentered moment. The recombination contribution is gamma(E[f((X+Y)/2)]-V). Mutation contributes E[b dot grad f] plus sigma squared times E[Laplacian f]/2. The finite-difference field defines the mutation operator itself; exact derivatives are used only to evaluate its generator on the known test objectives.

Save signed difference quotients, signed and absolute weak remainders ((V_out-V)/tau-g), normalized remainders divided by 1+V, both-order differences, and finite-step/generator sign disagreements. Retain every law, including positive drift and negative effective decay coefficients. Record the objective evaluations used to build and finite-difference-test the diagnostic support separately from closed-form derivative and expectation calculations. These are diagnostic costs, not an optimizer evaluation-complexity claim. No finite-population optimizer trajectory or particle-limit theorem is inferred from a frozen-law check.

## Calibration, validation and empirical constants

Use 32 independent calibration laws and 128 independent validation laws per instance and population family. Initial-law seed bases are 1,572,610,000 for calibration and 1,672,610,000 for validation, plus 1,000 times the instance index and the law index. Add 100,000 for the clustered family; the broad family uses no family offset. Smoke/unit-check laws use the separate base 1,772,610,000. Check disjointness of these sets and their separation from earlier experiment namespaces. Assemblies, orders and step sizes are paired on each input law.

For each instance and assembly, calculate lambda_cal as the minimum calibration value of -g/V across both population families, omitting only ratios with V<=1e-14 and reporting that guard count. Negative estimates remain negative. For each instance, assembly, order and step size, calculate a_cal(tau) as the maximum calibration normalized weak remainder |(V_out-V)/tau-g|/(1+V), again across both families. Thus each instance and assembly has one common empirical drift envelope and one common per-step remainder envelope for the two population geometries. These are finite-sample summaries, not uniform bounds or certified convergence constants. In particular, no quadratic remainder law is assumed: the general composition theorem requires only a remainder tending to zero.

Freeze these calibration summaries, source hashes and all parameters before the independent validation phase. Report all validation violations of g<=-lambda_cal V and of the per-step normalized-remainder envelope, both pooled and separately for each population family. A clean holdout comparison remains empirical evidence on these sampled laws, not proof for an invariant region, all laws or all times. No parameters, envelopes, law filters or step sizes are revised after validation. Retain negative and inconclusive findings.
