# Exploration-aware finite-step diagnostics

Fixed before the new full diagnostic run, 30 September 2026. This is an extension motivated by the one-step assembly results and the subsequent discussion, not an externally preregistered study or a new independent validation of previously chosen input-law distributions. The earlier manuscript, code, data, and figures remain unchanged.

## Scope and complete matrix

Retain every one of the ten problem/dimension instances, both population families, all 128 saved one-step validation input laws per family/instance, all eight operator assemblies, both orders (MRS and MSR), and all eight one-step step sizes. Use the saved input laws; do not select cases using new outcomes. The extension measures conditional one-step opportunities at a frozen population, not long trajectories, basin transitions, or global convergence.

For each input law and configuration, form exactly the same finite weighted selection/recombination output as in the one-step study. Draw candidates from this law and apply the declared mutation. Do not resample intermediate populations before completing the nonlinear selection/recombination composition. Split mutation diagnostically into its deterministic point x+tau*b(x) and its noise perturbation sigma*sqrt(tau)*Z; this is the exact implemented update, not an extra drift evaluation after the noise.

## Fixed numerical settings and coupling

- Each original law has N=32 equally weighted atoms.
- Use 256 conditionally iid candidate draws per input-law/configuration, partitioned into eight disjoint batches of B=32.
- Reuse uniform parent-selection draws and standard Gaussian draws across assemblies, orders, and step sizes at the same input law. For H-on versus H-off, the sampled pre-mutation parent and deterministic drift point are identical. Recombination/selection comparisons use inverse-CDF coupling of the finite support in its fixed original-atoms-then-midpoints order.
- Use a fresh candidate-randomness seed base 2,072,600,000, plus 100,000 times the instance index, 10,000 for the clustered family, and the input-law index. Smoke/unit tests use a distinct base 2,172,600,000. These streams must not overlap the existing experiment namespaces.
- Candidates are iid within each configuration conditional on its input law. Configurations are paired, and batches from the same input law share that law; neither constitutes independent evidence about an entire law class.

## Quantities to record

1. **Exact finite-step mean contributions.** Record the mean gap after each actual S/R stage, after deterministic drift, and after Gaussian mutation using the existing closed-form expectation. Successive changes telescope to the total mean change. The stages are order-dependent; they are not the same as generator actions evaluated at the original law. Inactive components have zero change. In particular, record the exact conditional noise cost separately.
2. **Outcome distributions.** Save per-batch mean objective changes and their normalized values, and retain favorable/adverse tails rather than only a median. For the paired noise comparison, record per-batch average f(Y_noisy)-f(Y_drift). Its expectation is the exact conditional noise cost.
3. **New-record discovery.** Freeze b=min_i f(x_i) at the original input population before any comparison. Record candidate probability of f(Y)<b, expected positive improvement (b-f(Y))_+, and the eight batch gains J=(b-min_l f(Y_l))_+. Continuous gains retain these exact positive-part definitions; classify a strict record only when b-f(Y)>eta, where eta=1e-12*(1+b), to avoid counting floating-point equality as discovery. Use the same eta for positive/negative/tied paired-event classification. Also record improvements of at least 10% of b (and exceeding eta) as a declared material-improvement sensitivity; report guards if b is numerically zero. Selection cannot itself create a new objective record because it only reweights existing support.
4. **Controlled component effects.** For each enabled switch H/R/S, compare J with its matched disabled-switch version at the same original population, order, and step size. Save signed gain differences, positive/negative/tied counts, and all quantiles. H pairing has the same pre-noise points; R/S effects include their interactions with subsequent operators. This is a specified counterfactual coupling, not a unique additive attribution or a claim that any individual beneficial trajectory was caused exclusively by one component.
5. **Spread and location.** Record the exact output total variance E||Y-EY||^2 and its change from the input. Report candidate nearest-input distance divided by the input median nearest-neighbor distance. Define a spatially new point for this diagnostic only as one farther than half that input scale from every original atom. Record its probability and its joint occurrence with a new objective record. This geometric threshold is fixed here and is not a definition of a new attraction basin; spread without objective improvement is not by itself a demonstrated benefit.
6. **Mean cost versus occasional benefit.** Report how often an enabled component's paired batch record improves when its exact paired mean objective cost is positive. For H, also record noise-only new-record events (noisy batch beats b while its matched drift-only batch does not), and their losses. No adverse cells or inconclusive zero-event cases are removed.

Additionally, at each original input law record the exact midpoint-recombination contribution distribution C_R=(f(X)+f(X'))/2-f((X+X')/2), using the product of the original parent weights. Report its weighted quantiles at the fixed levels below, positive/negative/zero probability masses, and positive/negative mean parts. Its mean is the negative unit-rate recombination generator action; the symmetric two-parent reference avoids choosing one arbitrary parent as the baseline.

Candidate objective evaluations, deterministic reference evaluations, finite-difference support costs, and analytic diagnostic calculations are recorded separately. These are diagnostic costs, not optimizer evaluation budgets. No success, basin, or regularity parameter is fitted using the new draws.

## Reporting and verification

Save input hashes, source/protocol hashes, seeds, exact phase means, and all per-law/per-batch aggregates sufficient to reconstruct the tables and paired comparisons. Large candidate arrays may be replaced by fixed histograms and candidate counts/positive-improvement sums per law, because candidates are reproducible from the saved seeds and inputs. Fix any histogram/quantile summaries before the full run; do not retain only favorable quantiles.

Present the entire instance/family/assembly matrix in data tables. The primary exploration figure uses the predeclared H-on/off contrast at tau=0.1, retaining every problem and both families and equally weighting the four S/R configurations and two orders. The step-size sensitivity uses every one of the eight step sizes, not a retrospectively chosen subset. Other figures may aggregate over assemblies but must state the aggregation and retain every problem and both families. Give distributions or intervals, not only average signs. At minimum report quantiles 0.05, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.95 and the mass at zero. If uncertainty intervals are used, resample input-law indices as clusters, retaining their paired configurations and batches.

Check component telescoping, agreement with the one-step study's exact output means, zero effects of inactive switches, same-parent H coupling, support bounds, nonnegative record gains, covariance/variance identities, seed disjointness, and complete-matrix coverage before the full run. Commit protocol and implementation after tests and before running the complete diagnostic panel. Preserve smoke outputs separately and retain all failures in the final audit.
